Overview

This project provides a comprehensive, field-informed analysis of water access, availability, and challenges in Huluul District, Sanaag Region. Huluul is an urban center administering approximately 18 surrounding villages, with a mixed population of urban residents and nomadic pastoralist communities.

The district is known for its natural resources (including gums/resins and minerals), yet faces significant water access challenges affecting both urban and rural populations.

Context

Although the Sanaag region is considered one of the most water-rich areas in Somaliland in terms of underground potential, these resources remain largely undeveloped. As a result:

Freshwater wells are extremely limited

Water infrastructure is underdeveloped

Communities rely on traditional and temporary water systems


Nomadic communities depend heavily on water trucking, while both villages and urban areas face shortages of reliable and clean water.

Problem Statement

Lack of freshwater wells

Dependence on berkads (water reservoirs) and seasonal water

Absence of sustainable water supply systems

Limited technical development of groundwater resources


Key Insight

Field observations suggest that groundwater exists but is not systematically developed. In some areas, shallow to medium-depth drilling (e.g., ~35 meters) may provide access to natural water sources if properly assessed.

Objectives

Analyze water access across urban, village, and nomadic communities

Identify infrastructure and accessibility gaps

Compare water availability across community types

Provide practical, data-driven recommendations


Study Area

Huluul Town (Urban)

18 surrounding villages

Nomadic pastoralist (rural) areas


Data Description

The dataset includes:

Water source types (Tap, Well, Berkad)

Distance to water points (km)

Number of users

Water quality

Community type (Urban, Village, Nomadic)


Methodology

1. Field-based contextual assessment


2. Data collection and structuring


3. Data cleaning and validation


4. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)


5. Comparative analysis (urban vs village vs nomadic)


6. Visualization and reporting



Recommendations (Strategic)

Conduct hydrogeological surveys to confirm groundwater potential

Develop shallow and medium-depth wells where feasible

In areas where well drilling is not viable or groundwater is unavailable, expand and improve berkad systems

Use berkads primarily as water storage (backup systems), not as the only water source

Invest in small-scale water systems for villages

Support mobile water solutions for nomadic communities

Improve berkad design, protection, and water quality management
